How to use it

  1. Open the official DataSF Building Permits dataset, i98e-djp9 and obtain a CSV export. Keep the column headers and the source dates. You can filter the official dataset before exporting a smaller file.
  2. Open the CSV as text, copy it with its header, and paste it above. The tool accepts display headers such as “Permit Number” and API-style names such as “permit_number”.
  3. Choose a filing cutoff or a scope filter, then select Review CSV. Separate modes cover hood context, refrigeration wording and coffee/tea context. Read the original scope before treating a match as useful.
  4. Download matched CSV for your own research. It retains permit IDs, source/status dates, reference candidates, duplicate flags, official record links and unverified purchasing status.

What the filters establish

The broad filter looks for restaurant, coffee shop, coffee bar, café, or walk-in cooler/freezer wording. The narrower filter also requires kitchen, hood, walk-in cooler/freezer, or equipment plan/schedule/layout wording. A generic equipment-room mention does not satisfy that narrower rule. These are text rules; they can miss relevant projects or include unrelated work. A cabinet-only job can still match the broad filter and is flagged for inspection.

The broad rule also accepts food-service, food-preparation, beverage-bar and commercial-kitchen wording. Every trade mode first requires food-service context. Cabinet-only exclusions do not pass trade modes. Hood context can mean equipment placed under an existing hood; it does not imply hood replacement. Refrigeration wording can describe removal rather than installation. Coffee/tea context does not establish a coffee-machine purchase.

When Primary Address Flag is supplied, rows explicitly marked secondary are skipped. Repeated permit IDs retain their first remaining row and receive a duplicate warning; conflicting versions need source review. A reference such as “PA# 202609037630” is a candidate relationship, not an automatic project merge. Distinct permit IDs are not counted as distinct buyers.

Files without source freshness or primary-address columns receive a warning. The tool does not infer missing owner names, contacts, suppliers, opening dates or procurement. The CSV export time records your download, not a fresh government update.
